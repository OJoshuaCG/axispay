# ADR-0057: Outgoing webhooks in Phase 5: outbox, delivery attempts, retries and SSRF protection

- **Status:** Proposed (Phase 5, slice A: backend only; the panel screens come in a later slice)
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **Source:** master plan sections 7.6, 15.1-15.7, 17.3, 22 and 27 (Phase 5); [ADR-0008](0008-outgoing-webhooks-standard-webhooks.md), [ADR-0051](0051-checkout-and-card-payments-phase-4.md) (business events recorded since Phase 4).

## Context

Since Phase 4 every business event (a link opened, a payment that succeeded or failed...) is recorded in the same transaction as the change, but nothing is sent. Phase 5 delivers these events to the merchants' endpoints, signed as Standard Webhooks, with retries and protection against requests to private networks. The plan leaves a few details open: how retries are stored, what a manual resend or a test does to the retry schedule and to the endpoint's health, and how the sweeper tells a lost job from one still waiting.

## Decision

1. **Publication in the same transaction.** When a business event is recorded, the same transaction stores its webhook event (the exact body that will be sent, frozen) and one pending delivery for every enabled endpoint of the tenant and mode subscribed to its type. The webhook event exists even when no endpoint is subscribed, for the event history of `GET /v1/events`. The jobs are queued only after the commit. A business event is published at most once.
2. **One row per attempt.** Every delivery attempt is its own row with its number, result, HTTP code, time, a sanitized excerpt of at most 2 KB of the answer and the kind of error (never an error message). A failed automatic attempt schedules the next one on the plan's calendar: immediately, then 5 s, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 5 h, 10 h and 10 h (8 attempts, about 27 hours); after the eighth the delivery is abandoned. A retry due within a minute is queued with a delay; later ones are queued by the sweeper when they are due.
3. **Manual resend and test event are single attempts.** A resend from the delivery log sends the same event (same body, same `webhook-id`, new timestamp and signature) once, and does not restart the calendar. The test event (`ping`) is sent immediately, waits at most the 10-second budget and returns its result to the panel; it can be sent to a disabled endpoint. Neither of them changes the endpoint's health.
4. **Endpoint health.** Only automatic attempts count. The first failure after a success starts the failure streak; any success ends it. After 5 days of continuous failures the endpoint is disabled (`disabled_by_failures`), audited, and the owners and the users with `webhooks:manage` get an e-mail. Pending attempts of a disabled or deleted endpoint are dropped when their turn comes. Enabling it again starts a new streak; only events created from then on are sent (missed ones can be resent). Likewise, a new endpoint only receives the events that happen once it exists: events published late by the sweeper (for example, recorded before Phase 5) never reach an endpoint registered after them.
5. **No double sends.** An attempt being sent holds a short lease; a duplicated job finds it taken or finished and does nothing. The sweeper (every minute) publishes business events left unpublished (for example, recorded before Phase 5) and queues due attempts whose job was never queued or was queued more than two minutes ago without running. Delivery is at least once; receivers deduplicate by `webhook-id`. A job that starts a moment before its attempt is due (clocks of different servers differ by a second or two) sends it; one that starts earlier goes back to the queue for the time left, instead of waiting for the sweeper.
6. **SSRF protection** at registration, when enabling, and before every attempt: `https` only (`http` only in test mode and only when the platform turns it on), no user or password in the URL, ports 443 and 8443, at most 2048 characters, a domain name (never an address in any notation), no `localhost`, `.local`, `.internal` or platform hosts, and every address the domain resolves to outside the private, loopback, link-local (cloud metadata), CGNAT, multicast, documentation and reserved ranges, including IPv6 forms that embed an IPv4 address. The request then connects only to the addresses that were checked (no second resolution) and never follows redirects. The address stored and called is written in one canonical form (lowercase domain, international domains in their ASCII form), so the name the request uses is exactly the name that was checked; a domain written with a final dot (`example.com.`) is refused, because it would escape that check. Looking up the domain counts towards the 10-second budget of the attempt: when the lookup uses it up, the attempt is a timeout and is retried like one. A blocked destination is recorded as `blocked_destination` and never retried; it still counts as a failure of the endpoint.
7. **Secrets.** Generated as `whsec_` + 32 random bytes, stored encrypted, shown once when created or rotated, and shown again only after re-authentication (audited). After a rotation the previous secret keeps signing for 24 hours and both signatures are sent. Jobs carry identifiers only.
8. **Permissions.** Everything needs `webhooks:manage`. Creating, editing, rotating, revealing, enabling and deleting also need re-authentication (plan 17.3). A read-only (suspended or closed) tenant can still disable and delete endpoints, nothing else. Creating, editing, rotating the secret, deleting and the automatic disabling e-mail the owners and the users with `webhooks:manage`; e-mails name the URL's host only.
9. **Scope-bypass whitelist:** `Webhooks\Services\WebhookOutboxLookup` finds, across tenants, the unpublished business events and the due delivery attempts (identifiers only); each one is then handled in its own tenant context ([ADR-0031](0031-tenancy-enforcement.md)).

## Consequences

- The domain lookup itself cannot be cut short by the platform: a single lookup lasts at most what the servers' own name-resolution settings allow (about 30 seconds with their defaults). The platform starts no further lookup once the budget is spent, and the delivery job allows for that worst case. Operations should keep the resolution timeout of the worker servers short (one or two seconds, a single retry).
- Deleting an endpoint deletes its delivery log; its events stay.
- An egress proxy or dedicated outgoing addresses (plan 15.7 point 6), the 30-day retention of the delivery log, `GET /v1/events`, the panel screens and the integrator documentation are later slices of Phase 5.
